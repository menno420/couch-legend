## 0 · Why these three, and why prompts before pictures

Chapters 4–6 open arc 2 — the first apartment, the room becoming a system,
the first Sunday rhythm — and they are the chapters a new player reaches
inside the first four hours, so they are seen by everyone who plays at all.
The arc-2 *anchor* (`public/art/couch-lucid.jpg`) is already a lived-in
room with lamp, plants and lava lamp, which is closer to chapter 5 than to
chapter 4: a first apartment needs to be **barer** than the anchor so the
room can visibly fill up across three chapters. That progression is the
point of the slice, and it is why the three are written together rather than
one at a time.

Prompts first: the method's own rule is *"a prompt cannot carry what a
contract should carry"* — the match comes from the named anchor files and
the invariants below, not from adjectives. A generated candidate is a dice
throw against this text; the text is what gets corrected when a candidate is
wrong.

## 1 · The shared preamble (paste above every lucid prompt)

```text
ANCHOR. Match the attached reference images exactly in finish, light
language and continuity: image A (couch-lucid.jpg) supplies the painterly
finish, the palette and THE COUCH — a battered rust-red velvet three-seater
with rounded arms, a torn right arm with exposed stuffing and a rough
mismatched dark-green patch. Image B (cousins-couch-lucid.jpg) supplies the
PROTAGONIST — an adult in their early twenties, slim, dark curly hair,
oversized sage-green hoodie, dark loose trousers, calm half-lidded
expression. Inherit finish, palette, couch and character. Do NOT inherit
either image's room, layout, props or second figure.

STYLE. Painterly realism with visible brushwork; warm practical light with
deep green-black shadows; restrained, literary, deadpan mood. NOT cartoon,
NOT anime, NOT vector, NOT photoreal, NOT a green colour filter, NOT neon.

CAMERA. Eye level, very slightly above, square to the couch, the couch
centred in the lower two thirds of the frame and the protagonist seated at
its centre. Keep the top fifth of the frame calm and low-detail (chapter
text sits there). Every important prop must survive a centred 4:5 crop.

STANDING NEGATIVES. No text, no letters, no logos, no brand marks, no UI,
no watermark, no border or frame. Any paper, label, screen or packaging is
blank or abstract. No lit joint, no smoke being inhaled, no drug
paraphernalia in use, no instruction of any kind; the protagonist is
clearly an adult and is simply sitting. No floating objects, no motion
blur, no sticker-like cut-outs.

FUNCTION. A viewer must be able to answer, by looking: what changed in this
person's life; what pays for it now (one clear work or money clue); where
the couch is and what state it is in; what came before (retained souvenirs)
and one small hint of what comes next. The couch must read as a silhouette
at thumbnail size.

ONE IMAGE ONLY. Portrait 3:4, at least 1086 × 1448 pixels, full-bleed.
```

## 2 · Chapter 4 · The Couch — `first-apartment`

Beat (content table): *"A first apartment, a first proper tray, the first
ridiculous paid errand. The couch has a home now."* Side texture (looks pass
§ 5): rent, snacks and the missing remote become equal emergencies.
Keepsake this chapter leaves: **The Spare Key**.

```text
SUBJECT. The same rust couch, now indoors for good: a small bare first
apartment at night, and the protagonist sitting in the middle of it looking
quietly pleased with a room that is finally theirs.

COMPOSITION. Left third: two cardboard moving boxes stacked, one open,
against a bare wall with a single empty picture hook. Centre: the couch
against the wall, the protagonist seated centre, one seat cushion beside
them lifted and askew as if something was just searched for under it.
Right third: a tall window with thin curtains, a fire escape and one
sodium-amber streetlamp outside. In front of the couch an upturned wooden
crate serves as the table; on it, a proper metal rolling tray (clean,
dented, empty), a spare door key on a plain ring, a set of car keys and a
folded paper delivery bag — the first paid errand. Pinned to the wall above
the couch, two small blank rectangles of paper: the kept job flyer and the
corner-store pay slip from the earlier chapters. One bare ceiling bulb on a
cord is the main light; the streetlamp adds a cool amber from the right.
Nothing else in the room — the emptiness is the story.

MATERIALS to render correctly: worn rust velvet and the green patch; raw
cardboard; the dull metal of the tray; a clear glass of water on the crate.

SILHOUETTE. The couch and the seated figure must read as one dark shape
against the pale bare wall at thumbnail size.

NEXT-CHAPTER HINT. A second, unfamiliar coat hook screwed into the wall by
the door with nothing on it yet.
```

## 3 · Chapter 5 · Rituals of the Room — `apartment-lived-in`

Beat: *"Water, snacks, a roommate, a playlist. The room becomes a small
system that runs without you."* Side texture: the remote is found three feet
from where the search began. Keepsake: **The Standing Glass**.

```text
SUBJECT. The same first apartment, months later, at lamp-lit evening: the
room has filled up and started running itself, and the protagonist is
sinking into the couch at its centre while a roommate crosses the frame at
the right edge.

COMPOSITION. The same wall, window and couch position as the first
apartment — the boxes are gone. Left third: a floor lamp with a warm
fabric shade and, beside it on a low shelf, a small green-and-orange lava
lamp and a potted trailing plant. Centre: the couch with a knitted blanket
over its left arm, the protagonist seated centre, hood down, a tall
standing glass of water in their hand. Right edge: the roommate — a second
adult, cropped by the frame, back mostly to us, carrying a bowl of snacks
toward the couch; their face is not the subject. A small speaker on the
shelf with a phone propped against it (screen blank). The crate has become
a real coffee table: on it a bowl of pretzels, the metal rolling tray with
the small green lighter resting on it, and the TV remote lying on the floor
three feet from the couch. Work clue: a folded pizza-delivery cap and a
small stack of blank flyers on the coat hook by the door. Retained
souvenirs: the two blank papers still pinned above the couch, plus a
corner-store name badge (blank) hung on the picture hook.

MATERIALS: lamp-gold fabric, the glass catching lamplight, wool blanket,
velvet, the lava lamp's glow on the wall behind it.

SILHOUETTE. Couch plus seated figure as one shape; the roommate is a dark
partial shape at the frame edge, never competing.

NEXT-CHAPTER HINT. A wall calendar with one weekend square circled, and low
late-afternoon light beginning at the very edge of the window.
```

## 4 · Chapter 6 · The Long Sunday — `sunday-light`

Beat: *"Work, smoke, come down, begin a better afternoon. The first visible
rhythm of a life."* Side texture: a wedding open-bar postcard introduces
alcohol as a social detour, never a higher tier. Keepsake: **The Sunday
Ledger**.

```text
SUBJECT. The same room on a late Sunday afternoon, long bars of low golden
sun coming through the window across the floor and the couch; the
protagonist reclined at the couch's centre in the calm after a week, a
little older, a notebook open on their knee.

COMPOSITION. The window on the right is now the light source: warm
horizontal sun bars with dust hanging in them, the lamp off. Left third: a
wooden chair with a work apron and a name badge (blank) folded over its
back — the week's work, put down. Centre: the couch, the blanket now on the
right arm, the protagonist reclined with feet up on the coffee table, a mug
beside them and a small notebook open on one knee with a pencil. The lava
lamp is unlit and the plant has grown. On the coffee table: the rolling
tray, the green lighter, the standing glass. Pinned to the wall above the
couch among the older papers: one new postcard-sized blank card with a
faint image of two glasses raised — the wedding detour. A wall calendar
with a repeating pattern of small marks on every Sunday.

MATERIALS: sunlight on velvet and floorboards; airborne dust; ceramic mug;
paper.

SILHOUETTE. The reclined figure and couch as one low shape cut by the
sun bars; the chair is the only other vertical.

NEXT-CHAPTER HINT. A closet door on the far left standing a hand's width
open, with a faint cool violet glow inside it and one leaf visible.
```

## 5 · The baked state — an edit of the lucid master, never a second generation

Per the delivered package § 8, the baked state is a composition-preserving
lighting edit of the lucid master, generated from the lucid image itself.

```text
Using the attached image as the master, produce its BAKED state. Keep
everything identical — the people, their pose and expression, the room
geometry, the couch, every prop, the camera and the crop. Change ONLY the
light and the air: [CHAPTER LINE]. Add fine suspended motes and two or three
restrained, dream-like marks — faint constellation lines or a soft
ring of light — in the upper part of the room only. Faces, hands and props
stay still and sharp. No green colour filter; no new objects; no text.
Output the same size and aspect as the master.
```

`[CHAPTER LINE]`, per chapter:

- **The Couch:** the bare bulb warms to honey and blooms softly; the
  streetlamp's amber creeps further across the floor; the window glass
  gathers faint reflections of a room fuller than this one.
- **Rituals of the Room:** the lamp's gold deepens and the lava lamp's green
  spills up the wall in slow ripples; the water in the standing glass
  catches a light that has no source.
- **The Long Sunday:** the sun bars turn from gold to rose-gold and widen;
  the dust becomes a slow glitter; the calendar's marks glow faintly, one
  after another.

## 7 · Acceptance — the questions, one per delivery

Answerable in seconds, per the method:

1. **Thumbnail test:** shrunk to a phone-icon size, do the couch and the
   seated figure still read as one silhouette?
2. **Is it the same couch?** Rust velvet, torn right arm, green patch —
   in the same place, the same size, as chapter 3's.
3. **Is it the same person, a little older?** Hair, hoodie, build.
4. **The four looks-pass questions** (what changed · what pays · where is
   the couch · what came before and what comes next) — each answerable
   without a caption.
5. **Any text, logo, or anything that reads as instruction?** One yes
   rejects the image.
6. **Baked pair:** are the people, props and crop pixel-stable against the
   lucid master, with only light and air changed?

## 8 · Route

The delivered arc-1 masters were generated as three separate 3:4 lucid
illustrations with the couch anchors attached as references, then each baked
state as a lighting edit of its own master (§ 8 of the arc-1 package). The
same route applies: **one asset per call**, references attached, lucid then
baked. Surfaces, per the estate's measurement (2026-08-04): with an anchor
image and this structure any of ChatGPT, Gemini or Grok converges; Gemini
won outright for expressive single images. The session that wrote this
records what it ran, and with what result, in § 9.
